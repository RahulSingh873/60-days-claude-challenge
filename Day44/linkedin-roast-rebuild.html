Headline 3/10

"B.Tech CSE'28| DSA with C++ | HTML| JavaScript | CSS | Blockchain | Solidity | 50+ Leetcode Questions | Deep Learning with AI"
The problem
This is a tag cloud, not a headline. A recruiter scanning search results in 3 seconds sees nine nouns fighting for attention and no sentence telling them what you actually are. Are you a DSA specialist? A blockchain dev? An AI person? You've made them guess, and recruiters don't guess — they scroll past.
Why it hurts
LinkedIn search ranks on relevance to the recruiter's query, not raw keyword count. Stuffing "Blockchain," "Solidity," and "Deep Learning" alongside each other actually dilutes your match score for any single role, because the algorithm can't tell which one is your primary identity.
Invisible cost
Walmart/JPMorgan/Deloitte recruiters searching "full-stack developer intern" or "software engineer intern" won't rank you highly, because nothing in your headline says that plainly.

About — first 2 lines 2/10

"👋 Hi, I'm Rahul Singh, a third-year B.Tech Computer Science student with a passion for technology, creativity, and continuous learning."
The problem
This is the single most valuable line on your entire profile — it's what shows before "see more" — and you spent it on the same sentence 100,000 other CS students have. "Passion for technology and continuous learning" is filler that says nothing about you specifically.
Why it hurts
Recruiters decide whether to click "see more" in under 2 seconds. A generic opener signals a generic candidate before they've read a single real fact about you.
Invisible cost
Most readers never click "see more." Everything below this line — your UBA leadership, your project work — is currently invisible to 80%+ of profile visitors.

Invisible cost
